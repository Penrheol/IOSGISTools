IOS GIS Coverage & Gap Analysis Tool DEMO

Business of Weather · generic observing-network representativeness & gap-mapping utility · built for TOR Phase 6 (Spatial Coverage & Network Adequacy Analysis)

This is a working demonstration tool. It opens with four illustrative sample stations (placed near 0°N, 0°E, clearly not real locations) so the map and calculations have something to show. All calculations run locally in your browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere.
A verified station dataset is available: WMO OSCAR/Surface, Jamaica (840 records, user-supplied export). Load it with the button in Station inventory below, or import your own OSCAR/Surface CSV export for any country. Currently loaded: illustrative sample data — not real station locations.

Coverage is estimated by sampling a regular grid across the area of interest and testing whether each sample point falls within the representativeness radius of at least one included station (great-circle distance). Grid resolution: 40 × 40 points. This is a generic geometric estimate, not a siting-standard compliance assessment — real assessments should factor in terrain, exposure, sensor type, and telecommunication reliability, consistent with the Phase 6 methodology described in the accompanying proposal.
Methodology & how to read this tool

Purpose. This tool supports Phase 6 (GIS, Spatial Coverage and Network Adequacy Analysis) of the IOS Gap Analysis methodology by giving reviewers a quick, reproducible way to visualise how a candidate station network relates to a defined area of interest, before country-specific GIS layers (administrative boundaries, population, hazard exposure, terrain) are assembled during the country visits.

Measurement classes. Class A–D follows the measurement-uncertainty grading used in Charting a Course for Sustainable Hydrological and Meteorological Observation Networks in Developing Countries (World Bank / Grimes et al., 2022), applied during Phase 4 (Inventory) of this assignment.

Coverage %. Computed by grid-sampling the bounding box of the area of interest and testing each sample point's distance to the nearest included station against the representativeness radius. Finer grids (more points) give a more precise estimate at the cost of computation time; 40×40 is a reasonable default for a country-level view.

Limits of this demo. It does not use real administrative boundaries, population, or hazard layers, and does not account for terrain, elevation, or line-of-sight for telemetry. It is a planning aid, not a substitute for the full spatial analysis (with verified station inventories and country GIS layers) to be carried out during Phase 6 of the assignment.
